 <div align="justify">
When discussing the Large-Scale Structure in recent decades, given the advancements in observational astronomy, we expect a very precise cosmological understanding. One of the important topics under investigation is the distribution of formed structures and the boundaries that will exist for the formation of these structures. Many results from these advanced observational observations will provide us with a detailed map of the recent cosmos. On the other hand, we know that different cosmological theories make different predictions about the statistics of structures and voids, such as the Excursion Set Theory (EST) and the Peaks Theory \citep{Paranjape_2012}.
</div>

 <div align="justify">
Despite various theories proposing the necessary gravity for structure formation, the most accepted one is Poisson gravity. Nevertheless, this gravity faces challenges and tensions in cosmology, prompting people to seek an alternative gravity capable of better justifying these challenges and tensions in the cosmos. One area I worked on during my master's degree, under the guidance of \textbf{Dr. Nima Khosravi}, \textbf{Dr. Farnik Nikakhtar}, and \textbf{Prof. Roya Mohayaee}, is the ``generalized \textbf{Monge-Ampère gravity at cosmological scales} instead of Poisson gravity". We aim to investigate the merger history of dark matter halos within the Excursion Set Theory framework, utilizing the Monge-Ampère Gravity (MAG).
</div>

 <div align="justify">
Monge-Ampère gravity manifests as a nonlinear form of Poisson gravity, providing the same gravitational potential in small potential regimes or at the very large distance. MAG derived from the Lagrangian system and mass consistency, and the potential Laplacian, we determine the redshifts at which trajectories pass through the barrier of critical density, leading to the collapse and formation of virialized dark matter halos. Subsequently, we calculate halo statistics.
</div>

 <div align="justify">
Our interest and motivation in this gravity may owe much to these articles \cite{Brenier_2003,Frisch_2002,Nikakhtar_2022}. Particularly, the introduction of a mathematical tool and algorithm called \textbf{``Optimal Transport"} successfully simulated the structures formed, termed cosmic webs, using Monge-Ampère gravity. The obtained results, compared to Poisson gravity, were significantly noteworthy, prompting a more specialized investigation into Monge-Ampère gravity.
</div>

 <div align="justify">
Therefore, initially, we extracted the force from the Monge-Ampère equation. Assuming a test particle in spherical symmetrically distributed space, we derived the force for Monge-Ampère. A critical constraint we imposed was that at very far distances (towards infinity), the behavior of Monge-Ampère force aligns perfectly with Poisson force. Despite challenges, we managed to extract the resultant force.
</div>

 <div align="justify">
The next steps involved investigating the effects of Monge-Ampère gravity on halo statistics. We began by obtaining the critical density for structure formation in the nonlinear regime, aiming to discuss the formation of small-scale structures such as galaxies and galaxy clusters. So, to start in the nonlinear regime, according from Newton's second law, we solved equations, particularly utilizing the Runge-Kutta method for numerical solutions for MAG. To validate our algorithm, we performed numerical steps for Poisson gravity, known to produce a cycloid curve, unlike the case for Monge-Ampère. Ultimately, for Monge-Ampère, we obtained almost the same result as Poisson, indicating that structures initially move away from each other and then collapse due to gravitational constraints. However, it was found that the virial radius in Monge-Ampère is larger than in Poisson, meaning that celestial bodies in a given redshift require more time in Monge-Ampère gravity compared to Poisson gravity to form structures [As you can see in Figure 2 of the article \cite{bonnefous2024mongeamperegravityoptimaltransport}, which is the result of Prof. Bruno Lévy's simulation efforts].
</div>

 <div align="justify">
Though successful in extracting the density variance, challenges persist in solving it in the linear regime, currently under the investigation of Prof. Roya Mohayaee. Nonetheless, extracting halo statistics isn't arduous, aided by existing theories such as EST, enabling the plotting of a power spectrum. Future work involves examining mass function profiles and obtaining final halo statistics in various redshifts, necessitating the adoption of tools like Optimal Transport to simulate cosmic webs for MAG and comparison with simulations conducted in Poisson gravity.
</div>

Additional motivations for pursuing this gravity include:
1. Non-Linear Modification of the Poisson equation,
1. Predicted by Statistical Physics,
1. Its Non-Divergent behavior,
1. Absence of Free Parameters (Numerical and Physical),
1. and Capturing Anisotropic Collapse.

 <div align="justify">
Alongside these, we thoroughly examined Monge-Ampère gravity and its density contrast evolution equations in Fourier space, leading to the extraction of the growth factor. Moreover, we solved the potential resulting from this gravity in different dimensions and spherical coordinates, detailed in my thesis in Persian, awaiting translation and completion.
</div>
